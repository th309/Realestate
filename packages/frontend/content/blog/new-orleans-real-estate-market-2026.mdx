New Orleans is one of the most culturally distinct cities in the United States. Its real estate market, however, is one of the most challenged in the country by the data.

[PropertyIQ scores New Orleans a 25 out of 100](/markets/new-orleans-metairie-la) as of February 28, 2026. That places it in the bottom tier of markets nationally. Home values have declined 6.5% year over year. Homes are sitting on the market for nearly three months on average. The Zillow one-year price forecast is -3.8%.

This post covers what the data shows, without editorializing about whether or why conditions may change.

## Where the Score Came From

The PropertyIQ Score for New Orleans has been deeply challenged for an extended period:

- February 2026: 25
- January 2026: 32
- December 2025: 27
- October 2025: 28
- August 2025: 28
- June 2025: 25
- April 2025: 22
- February 2025: 23
- December 2024: 19
- October 2024: 10
- August 2024: 8

All data as of the dates listed. The score has risen from a low of 8 in August 2024 to 25 in February 2026. That is improvement. It is also still 25 out of 100.

The January 2026 peak of 32 followed by a drop back to 25 in February indicates this recovery is not linear or stable.

## Home Values Are Declining

Home values in New Orleans have fallen 6.53% year over year as of February 2026. The Zillow estimated home value is approximately $253,685 as of January 2026. The median listing price is $299,000 as of February 2026.

The five-year return for New Orleans real estate is -4.91% as of February 2026. This means homeowners who purchased five years ago have less nominal equity today than they did at purchase, before accounting for inflation. Negative five-year appreciation is unusual and represents a meaningful data point for anyone evaluating the market.

Zillow's 12-month price forecast as of December 2025 is -3.8%. If that projection is accurate, nominal values will continue to decline through early 2027.

## Market Absorption Is Very Slow

For-sale inventory in New Orleans is 3,790 active listings as of February 2026, up 2.68% year over year. That is modest inventory growth by itself. The absorption picture is what tells the story.

Pending listings stand at only 797 as of February 2026, against 3,790 active. That is a pending ratio of 21%. Compare this to high-scoring markets like Grand Rapids, where the pending ratio is 86%. In New Orleans, fewer than one in four listed homes has a buyer.

Days on market is 88 as of February 2026. Homes are sitting for nearly three months on average before going under contract. This is among the higher DOM readings among major Southern metros.

New listings are actually declining: down 11.41% year over year as of February 2026, with 854 new listings entering the market. Sellers are choosing not to list. That choice limits fresh supply but does not address the absorption problem on existing inventory.

## Price Cuts Are Elevated

The price cut rate in New Orleans is 14.62% as of February 2026. Nearly 15% of active listings have reduced their asking price. The sale-to-list ratio is 97.36% as of November 2025, meaning homes that do sell are closing at roughly 2.6% below asking price on average.

Together, these figures indicate that the pricing environment is soft. Sellers who want to transact are accepting less than they asked. Sellers who are not willing to discount are contributing to the high days-on-market figure.

## The Income and Affordability Context

Median household income in New Orleans is $62,271 as of 2023 Census data. That is lower than most comparable Southern metros and significantly below the national median. The income required to purchase a home at current median prices is approximately $79,473 as of February 2026.

The gap between median income and income required to buy is meaningful but not the largest in the country. The estimated affordable home price for a median-income household is $234,281 as of February 2026, against a median listing price of $299,000. That $64,719 gap is real but not insurmountable for buyers with savings or assistance.

The market is 16% overvalued relative to local income fundamentals as of February 2026. That overvaluation persists even as home values are declining, which indicates that income growth has not kept pace with the elevated price levels that were established during the post-pandemic run-up.

Homeownership rate in New Orleans is 60.31% as of 2023, which is below national averages and reflects a metro where renting is a more prevalent tenure choice.

## The Rent Side

The Zillow Observed Rent Index for New Orleans is approximately $1,576 per month as of December 2025. With the Zillow home value at $253,685 and median listing prices around $299,000, the rent-to-price ratio can look attractive at the surface level for investors.

The challenge is that a declining value environment and slow absorption reduce the investment calculus. Rent income needs to cover not just carrying costs but potential value erosion if the trend continues. The 5-year return of -4.91% on home values is a data point every investor in this market should model.

Home sales via Zillow totaled 1,041 as of December 2025. New construction sales were 77 as of November 2025. These are relatively low transaction volumes for a metro of 988,000 people.

## The Economic Picture

Unemployment in New Orleans is 4.2% as of November 2025. That is slightly above national averages. The metro's economy is built around tourism, energy, healthcare, and port activity. These sectors carry different risk profiles than the technology and financial services anchors of higher-scoring metros.

Population is 988,763 as of 2023 Census data, reflecting a metro that has not returned to pre-Hurricane Katrina population levels two decades later. The long-run population trajectory is a structural factor the PropertyIQ Score does not fully capture but investors should research independently.

## What the Score Is Telling You

A PropertyIQ Score of 25 reflects a market where current conditions are measurably weak: home values are declining, absorption is slow, price cuts are elevated, and the one-year price forecast projects further decline.

That does not mean New Orleans is without any opportunity. Declining values and a low 21% pending ratio may create conditions where patient buyers with long time horizons and strong financial positions find properties at lower prices than recent years. But that is a property-level and individual financial evaluation, not a market-level endorsement.

The PropertyIQ Score reflects market conditions as of February 28, 2026. No score predicts what happens next. Individual decisions require property-specific analysis, legal and financial advice, and an honest assessment of personal risk tolerance.
