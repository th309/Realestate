The Norwich, CT real estate market scores 98 out of 100 on the PropertyIQ Index as of February 28, 2026. That puts Norwich in the top 2% of all U.S. metro areas tracked by PropertyIQ. For context: Phoenix, AZ scores 45. Austin, TX scores in the mid-40s range. The markets most investors focus on score far below this small Connecticut metro.

This post uses live PropertyIQ data, Zillow market metrics, and Realtor.com supply figures to explain what is driving Norwich's score and what the key indicators show. All data is effective as of the dates listed for each metric.

## What the PropertyIQ Score Measures

The PropertyIQ Score runs from 0 to 100 and is a forward-looking prediction of how a market is likely to perform over roughly the next three years relative to its state, where 50 is the state average. It is built on the market's demand signal: 12-month and 3-month home-value momentum, median days on market, and the share of listings cutting price, updated monthly. A score of 98 means the model expects Norwich to substantially outperform the typical Connecticut market.

Norwich's 98 is driven by three compounding factors: a historic collapse in new listings, demand that has outpaced available supply to an unusual degree, and appreciation momentum that has remained durable through the broader market slowdown of 2024 and 2025.

## The Pending Ratio Is the Number That Stands Out

Most housing market metrics tell you where things have been. The pending-to-active ratio tells you where things are headed.

A ratio above 1.0 means there are more buyers under contract than active listings available. That is not a normal market condition. It means every new listing that comes to market is already being absorbed by waiting buyers. In the Norwich metro, 302 listings were pending as of March 1, 2026, against only 267 active listings.

That 1.13 ratio is the clearest signal in the Norwich data. It explains why only 5.06% of listings saw price reductions: sellers do not need to discount when demand exceeds supply at the moment of listing. It also explains the 100.33% sale-to-list ratio, meaning homes are selling above asking price on average.

## New Listings Dropped Nearly 20% Year Over Year

The supply side of the Norwich equation is contracting sharply. New listings fell 19.85% year over year as of March 1, 2026. This is not a gradual drift. It is a meaningful acceleration of the supply shortage that has characterized New England real estate since 2020.

New construction is a limited relief valve. The Norwich area's housing stock is predominantly older, single-family homes on established lots. Zoning, land availability, and construction economics constrain new development. With 218 new listings per month against 302 pending buyers, each monthly wave of new listings is consumed before supply can accumulate.

The result: 267 total active listings for a metro area of 279,000 people. That is roughly one listing for every 1,045 residents. Nationally, a healthy market typically sustains a rate of one listing per 300 to 500 residents.

## Why Norwich Scores Above Coastal Connecticut Markets

Connecticut real estate is not uniform. Fairfield County markets (Greenwich, Westport, Darien) carry median prices well above $1 million and serve a New York City commuter demographic. Hartford's urban core scores differently from its suburbs. New Haven's market reflects Yale University dynamics.

Norwich sits in New London County, roughly 90 miles from New York and 45 miles from Hartford. Its price point, at a $432,075 median listing, is significantly below Fairfield County, which creates a different buyer pool: households working in healthcare, defense contracting, and regional finance who cannot access coastal pricing but maintain stable employment.

The presence of Naval Submarine Base New London, located in nearby Groton, is a structural anchor for the metro's housing demand. The base employs thousands of active-duty personnel, civilians, and defense contractors, and that employment base does not track national economic cycles the way commercial sectors do. Military presence creates consistent household formation, steady rental demand, and a buyer pool that includes military families using VA loan eligibility at a time when conventional mortgage affordability is stretched.

## What the Demand Score of 99.33 Means

Realtor.com calculates a demand score that measures buyer interest, page views, saves, and engagement relative to the number of active listings. A demand score of 99.33 out of 100 indicates that Norwich ranks in the top fraction of a percent nationally on buyer interest relative to available supply.

This metric is useful because it captures latent demand: buyers actively searching, not just people who have already made offers. When pending ratios and demand scores both confirm the same story, the signal is consistent.

## The Overvaluation Reading and What It Does Not Mean

The overvaluation figure of 35.8% deserves explanation. PropertyIQ calculates overvaluation by comparing current median prices to what local incomes can support at standard debt-to-income thresholds. When prices exceed what the local income base can comfortably afford, the model flags an overvaluation percentage.

Norwich's 35.8% overvaluation reading reflects a real affordability gap. The income needed to buy at current prices is $114,747 as of March 1, 2026, against a median household income of $84,185. That is a meaningful stretch.

It does not, however, mean the market is about to correct. Overvaluation in this context reflects the cost of living in a supply-constrained area with strong demand. The difference between a market that is overvalued and heading for correction and one that is overvalued and holding is whether demand can continue to meet constrained supply. The pending ratio, the demand score, and the supply trend all suggest demand is not retreating in the near term.

## Five-Year Appreciation Context

The 5-year appreciation figure of +32.97% as of March 1, 2026 places Norwich among the more durable appreciation markets in New England. This is not a speculative surge: it accumulated over five years with a relatively consistent pace, which is a different pattern from markets that appreciated 40% in 18 months and then corrected.

Zillow's home price forecast of +4.8% for the next 12 months (as of December 31, 2025) is consistent with the current supply/demand dynamics. Markets with pending ratios above 1.0 and demand scores near 100 typically continue to see price appreciation until either supply expands or demand retreats.

## The Rental Market in Norwich

For landlords, the $1,871 monthly rent figure (Zillow, December 31, 2025) against a median home value of $398,941 produces a gross rent yield of approximately 5.6% annualized. This is above the national median for single-family rentals, which has compressed toward 4.5% to 5.0% in many markets as home prices outpaced rent growth.

The income-to-rent figure of $74,835 represents the annual income needed to rent at current rates without exceeding standard affordability thresholds. Against a median income of $84,185, rent is within range for the median household, which supports continued rental demand.
