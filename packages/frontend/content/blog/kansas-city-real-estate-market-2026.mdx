While major Sun Belt metros absorb inventory overhangs and Northeast cities attract attention for their extreme scores, Kansas City, Missouri is doing something quietly interesting: producing consistent, broad-based real estate performance without attracting the speculation that has destabilized other markets.

PropertyIQ scores [Kansas City a 66 out of 100](/markets/kansas-city-mo-ks) as of February 28, 2026.

Here is what the data shows.

## Prices Are Rising

Median home values in Kansas City are up 4.09% year over year as of February 2026. Month-over-month, values gained 3.94% in February alone. Zillow forecasts an additional 2.7% near-term appreciation as of December 2025 — among the stronger forecasts for mid-tier metros.

## Homes Are Selling at Full Asking Price

The sale-to-list ratio in Kansas City is 100% as of November 2025. Sellers are receiving exactly what they list for, on average. This is a meaningful contrast to markets like [Tampa](/markets/tampa-st-petersburg-clearwater-fl) (97.5% of ask) or [Jacksonville](/markets/jacksonville-fl) (97.3%).

## Demand Is Solid

Kansas City's demand score is 60.5 out of 100 as of February 2026. The pending-to-active ratio is 0.7463 — nearly three-quarters of active listings are under contract at any given time. Homes average 57 days on market.

## Low Price Cuts

Only 10.77% of Kansas City listings have had a price reduction as of February 2026. That is one of the lower rates among major metros.

## The Valuation Picture Is Favorable

Kansas City is only 8.8% overvalued relative to local fundamentals as of February 2026 — a fraction of what [Phoenix](/markets/phoenix-mesa-chandler-az) (49.2%), [Las Vegas](/markets/las-vegas-henderson-north-las-vegas-nv) (64.7%), or [Seattle](/markets/seattle-tacoma-bellevue-wa) (85.2%) show. The Zillow median home value is approximately $315,784 as of January 2026. The median household income is $81,927 (2023 Census). The math is tighter than in Northeast markets, but it is workable.

## Unemployment Is Low

Kansas City's unemployment rate is 3.5% as of December 2025, matching Phoenix but with a far more favorable valuation picture.

## Why the Score Is 66 and Not Higher

Inventory is rising: Kansas City has 5,060 homes for sale as of February 2026, up 19.72% year over year. New listings jumped 26.81% year over year. This supply increase is the primary drag on an otherwise healthy market. If demand can absorb the new inventory, the score should hold or improve. If inventory continues to accelerate, the supply-demand balance could shift.
