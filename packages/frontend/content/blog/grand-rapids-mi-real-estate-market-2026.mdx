Grand Rapids, Michigan does not appear on most national investor shortlists. It is not a coastal market. It is not a Sun Belt story. It is a mid-sized Midwest metro of 1.15 million people that consistently outscores markets three times its size on every measurable indicator.

[PropertyIQ scores Grand Rapids a 93 out of 100](/markets/grand-rapids-wyoming-kentwood-mi) as of February 28, 2026. That is an A grade. The score has held between 89 and 96 for twelve consecutive months. This is not a one-month data event. It is a structural condition.

Here is what is driving it.

## Supply Is Tighter Than It Appears

The headline inventory number in Grand Rapids is 1,136 active listings as of February 2026. That sounds like a manageable figure for a metro of this size. Look at the direction of travel: inventory is down 4.7% year over year. New listings are down 7.44% year over year, with only 746 new properties hitting the market in February 2026.

Fewer sellers are choosing to list. The pipeline is thinning in a market that was already lean.

The pending ratio tells you the full picture: 979 homes under contract against 1,136 active listings as of February 2026. That is an 86% pending ratio. For every 100 homes on the market, 86 already have buyers. This is not a market where properties sit.

## Homes Are Moving at Full Price

Days on market in Grand Rapids is 64 as of February 2026. That is not fast by 2021 standards, but it reflects a market where sellers have leverage. Homes are selling at or near asking: the sale-to-list ratio sits at 99.83% as of November 2025.

Only 10.62% of listings have taken a price cut as of February 2026. That is one of the lower price reduction rates among Midwest metros. Sellers are not capitulating.

## Prices Are Rising, Not Correcting

The median listing price in Grand Rapids is $399,900 as of February 2026. The Zillow estimated home value is approximately $342,610 as of January 2026. Home values have risen 3.94% year over year as of February 2026.

The five-year appreciation figure is 27.99% as of February 2026. This is consistent, compounding growth, not a speculative spike followed by correction.

At $221 per square foot as of February 2026, Grand Rapids offers significant pricing efficiency compared to coastal and Sun Belt metros with comparable economic fundamentals.

## The Income and Affordability Picture

Median household income in the Grand Rapids metro is $80,296 as of 2023 Census data. The calculated income required to purchase a home at current prices is $106,292 as of February 2026. That gap reflects a housing affordability challenge that is real but manageable compared to markets where the income-to-purchase requirement exceeds $150,000 or $200,000.

The market is 20.5% overvalued relative to local income fundamentals as of February 2026. That is a moderate premium, not an extreme one. The estimated affordable home price for median-income households is $302,096 as of February 2026.

Homeownership rate in the metro is 74.31% as of 2023. That is high relative to national averages, reflecting a market where residents are committed buyers rather than renters.

## Rents and the Investment Case

The Zillow Observed Rent Index for Grand Rapids is approximately $1,637 per month as of December 2025. With home values in the low-to-mid $300,000s, this produces a rent-to-price ratio that compares favorably to most Sun Belt markets that saw explosive value growth in 2021 to 2023.

New construction sales totaled 98 units as of November 2025. This is a relatively constrained pipeline for a metro that continues to absorb demand at pace. Limited new supply additions are one reason the existing inventory pressure continues.

Zillow's 12-month home price forecast as of December 2025 is +4%. That projection, combined with current rent yields and low vacancy indicators, is why Grand Rapids attracts investors who prioritize risk-adjusted returns over headline market names.

The unemployment rate in the Grand Rapids metro is 4.0% as of November 2025. That is slightly above the national rate at that time but reflects a diversified manufacturing and services economy that has shown stability. The metro's economy includes healthcare, manufacturing, education, and a growing technology sector.

Population is 1,154,320 as of 2023 Census data. Median age is 36.6, one of the younger profiles among Midwest metros, which supports continued household formation and housing demand.
