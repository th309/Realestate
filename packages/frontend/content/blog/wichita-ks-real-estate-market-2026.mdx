Wichita, Kansas scores a 76 out of 100 on the PropertyIQ index as of February 28, 2026. The score is notable, but a single number buried in this data is more notable: the market is 11.8% undervalued relative to local income fundamentals. In a national environment where nearly every major metro is overvalued, Wichita is priced below what the local income base can support.

[PropertyIQ scores Wichita a 76 out of 100](/markets/wichita-ks) as of February 28, 2026. Here is what the data shows.

## Undervalued in a Overvalued Market Environment

The calculated overvaluation figure for Wichita is -11.8% as of February 2026. Negative means undervalued: current home prices are below what the local income fundamentals suggest they should support.

The median household income in Wichita is $68,930 (Census, 2023). The estimated income required to purchase at current prices is $71,598. That is a gap of less than $3,000 between what the median household earns and what is needed to qualify for a home purchase. For context, most major metros have income-to-purchase gaps of $30,000 to $100,000 or more.

The estimated affordable home price for a median-income household is $259,334 as of February 2026. The Zillow estimated home value is $214,794 as of January 31, 2026. Median-income households can afford more than the median home costs.

## What $146 Per Square Foot Means

Price per square foot in Wichita is $146 as of February 2026. That is one of the lower readings among scored U.S. metros. For investors used to $250 to $400 per square foot in Sun Belt or coastal markets, the entry cost difference is structural, not cyclical.

The median listing price is $269,373 as of February 2026. The income required to buy a home is $71,598. That combination is rare in the current market.

## Demand Is Absorbing Supply

For-sale inventory rose 9.68% year-over-year to 1,598 homes as of February 2026. New listings increased 15.74% to 794. Supply is growing, but demand is keeping pace.

The pending count of 836 against 1,598 active listings produces a pending ratio of 52.3%. That is a healthy absorption rate. More than half of the active inventory already has buyers.

Home sales grew 11.39% year-over-year as of February 2026. The sale-to-list ratio is 99.95% as of November 2025, essentially full price. Price cuts affect only 11.6% of listings, one of the lowest readings in this batch.

Days on market is 64 as of February 2026.

## The Aviation Economy

Wichita's economy is built around aerospace and aviation manufacturing. Boeing, Textron Aviation (Cessna and Beechcraft), Spirit AeroSystems, and Learjet all have significant operations in the metro. Wichita is sometimes called the Air Capital of the World, and the employment base reflects it.

That manufacturing concentration creates both durability and concentration risk. The economy performs well when aerospace is healthy and faces headwinds when the sector contracts. For buyers evaluating long-term hold scenarios, understanding the Boeing and Cessna employment cycles is relevant.

Unemployment in Wichita was 3.4% as of November 2025. That is below the national average and reflects the current health of the aerospace sector.

## Rent and the Investment Case

The Zillow Observed Rent Index for Wichita was $1,150 per month as of December 31, 2025. At a Zillow home value of $214,794, this produces a gross rent multiplier of approximately 15.6x. That is the most favorable rent-to-price ratio in this batch and one of the more compelling yield environments among scored U.S. metros.

New construction sales totaled 63 units as of November 2025. The five-year appreciation figure is 10.82% as of February 2026.

Zillow's 12-month home price forecast as of December 2025 is +3.1%.

The listing price declined 6.94% year-over-year as of February 2026, which appears to conflict with the undervaluation reading. The divergence reflects the difference between asking prices (which can fluctuate based on what sellers list at) and the underlying Zillow value estimate, which is more stable. The Zillow value at $214,794 is the more reliable baseline for structural analysis.

## Demographics and Population

Population in the Wichita metro is 648,935 (Census, 2023). Median age is 36.6. Homeownership rate is 64.68%.

The metro is mid-sized and does not attract the national investor attention of a Nashville or Phoenix. That relative obscurity is part of what keeps prices at $146 per square foot when comparable employment fundamentals in more visible metros support $250 or more.

## What the Score Does Not Tell You

A PropertyIQ Score of 76 means the model expects Wichita to outperform the typical Kansas market over the next few years, built from the market's demand signal — home-value momentum, days on market, and price-reduced share — as of February 28, 2026. It does not predict the trajectory of Boeing contracts, government defense spending, or regional migration patterns that could shift Wichita's economic base in either direction.

Any buyer or investor evaluating Wichita should conduct their own due diligence on local submarket conditions, employer concentration risk, and property-specific financials before making any decisions.

## The Bottom Line

Wichita scores 76 out of 100 on the PropertyIQ index as of February 28, 2026. It is 11.8% undervalued, priced at $146 per square foot, and requires only $71,598 in income to purchase at the median. Rent-to-price is 15.6x. Unemployment is 3.4%. Sales are up 11.39% year-over-year at full asking price.

In a market cycle where most metros are overvalued and cash flow is thin, those numbers stand out.
