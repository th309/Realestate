The Cape Coral FL real estate market 2026 scores 25 out of 100 on the PropertyIQ index as of February 28, 2026. A score of 25 places this market in the bottom tier nationally, with conditions across supply, demand, affordability, and velocity well below the national average.

The story is not purely negative. Six months ago, in September 2025, Cape Coral scored 9. The score hit a low of 6 in July 2025 before beginning to recover. The 16-point gain from that floor is real movement, and the recovery trajectory matters for anyone assessing whether Cape Coral represents a distressed opportunity or a market to avoid.

This post presents the data without a conclusion. What you do with a score of 25 depends entirely on your entry price, time horizon, and tolerance for a market that remains in a prolonged correction.

## What a Score of 25 Means

The PropertyIQ Score is a 0 to 100 composite index. A score of 50 represents the national average. A score of 25 is in the bottom quarter of all tracked markets. Conditions are unfavorable relative to most of the country.

The grade is F.

The factors that produce a score of 25: home values are down 9.1% year over year as of February 2026. That is the only market in this batch with a meaningful year-over-year price decline. Days on market of 83 means homes are sitting for nearly three months on average. The price cut percentage is 25.34%, meaning 1 in 4 sellers is reducing their asking price. Inventory is at 12,677 homes, though it is down 10.12% year over year, a supply contraction that is part of why the score has improved from its low.

## What Happened to Cape Coral

Cape Coral and the Fort Myers metro experienced one of the more dramatic boom-and-bust sequences in the post-pandemic real estate cycle.

The market surged during the pandemic as remote workers, retirees, and investors targeted Southwest Florida for its affordability relative to Southeast Florida, its waterway access, and its warm climate. Between 2020 and 2022, home values in Cape Coral rose faster than nearly any comparable market in the United States. The PropertyIQ platform data shows the score history going back through 2024 at levels in the low teens to low twenties, suggesting the correction was already well underway before the 2025 low.

Two compounding forces drove the deterioration. Insurance costs in Southwest Florida rose dramatically following hurricanes, with many carriers exiting the market or raising premiums to levels that changed the fundamental economics of ownership. Second, the inventory of new construction that was initiated during the boom began delivering into a market where buyer demand had already softened.

The result is a market with 12,677 homes for sale, homes taking 83 days to sell, and prices down 9.1% year over year. Home sales are actually up 24.37% year over year as of February 2026, which is the most positive signal in the dataset and a primary driver of the score recovery from 9 to 25.

## The Insurance Factor

No analysis of Cape Coral is complete without addressing insurance. Florida's property insurance market is in structural difficulty, and Southwest Florida is among the most affected regions in the state.

Insurance premiums for coastal and canal-front properties in Cape Coral have risen substantially since 2022, with many properties seeing annual premiums that have doubled or tripled. For investors, those costs directly reduce net operating income. For owner-occupants, they raise the total cost of ownership beyond what the mortgage payment alone reflects.

PropertyIQ's scoring incorporates price-to-income ratios and affordability metrics, but it does not directly capture insurance cost escalation as a line item. Investors evaluating Cape Coral should independently assess current insurance premiums for any specific property or property type under consideration. What looks like a 5.3% gross rent yield at the listing price may be significantly thinner after insurance, property management, and maintenance costs specific to Florida.

The Zillow home value for Cape Coral was $340,227 as of January 31, 2026. The rent index was $1,859 per month as of December 31, 2025. That produces a gross rent yield of approximately 6.6%. Florida insurance premiums can consume 1% to 2% or more of home value annually for certain property types in this market, which would bring the effective yield down meaningfully.

## The Recovery Signal

The score movement from 9 to 25 over six months is the data point that separates Cape Coral from a market in freefall.

Home sales rose 24.37% year over year as of February 2026. That is the highest sales growth in this batch of five markets. Buyers are returning to Cape Coral, drawn by price reductions and improving relative affordability against where the market was in 2022.

Inventory fell 10.12% year over year, meaning the supply overhang is contracting. New listings fell 21.55% year over year. Fewer sellers are entering the market, which is allowing existing inventory to clear at a more sustainable rate.

The pending listings count of 2,996 as of February 2026 is nearly equal to the new listings count of 2,956. That near-equilibrium between new supply and demand absorption suggests the market is approaching balance at the current price level, even if that level is well below the 2022 peak.

The sale-to-list ratio was 95.88% as of November 2025. That means buyers are getting approximately 4% below asking price on average. That is the most buyer-favorable sale-to-list ratio in this batch, and it represents real negotiating leverage that did not exist two years ago.

## Price Level and Affordability

The Zillow home value of $340,227 as of January 31, 2026 is down from levels above $400,000 at the market peak. That correction has improved affordability in absolute terms, though the market remains above what a pure income-fundamental analysis would support.

PropertyIQ estimates Cape Coral homes are 31.6% overvalued relative to long-run fundamentals as of February 2026. That overvaluation figure coexists with a 9.1% year-over-year price decline, meaning the correction has not yet closed the gap between prices and fundamentals. The market peaked at overvaluation levels far above 31.6% and is working its way down.

The income required to afford a median-priced home is $106,292 annually as of February 2026. The metro's median household income was $73,099 as of 2023 Census data. That gap is real, though the sale-to-list ratio at 95.88% means buyers with sufficient income are extracting meaningful discounts from listing prices.

The median age in Cape Coral is 49.3, the highest in this batch and consistent with a retirement-destination market. That demographic profile creates a buyer and seller base that is less urgently income-constrained than a workforce-demographic market, but it also means demand is more sensitive to broad retirement-income dynamics and less anchored to local employment.

## 5-Year Context

The 5-year home price growth in Cape Coral was 24.36% as of February 2026. That figure reflects both the massive run-up and the subsequent correction. A buyer who purchased in 2021 at the peak has seen values decline. A buyer who purchased in 2019 or 2020 before the surge still holds meaningful gains.

The Zillow 12-month home price forecast for Cape Coral is +0.4% as of December 2025. That is the most cautious forecast in this batch, consistent with a market that is stabilizing rather than appreciating. The forecast does not suggest further deterioration, but it does not signal a recovery rally either.

## Who Should and Should Not Consider Cape Coral

Cape Coral is not a market for every investor or buyer. The score of 25 is a real signal that conditions remain unfavorable by most measures. The specific cases where Cape Coral may warrant evaluation:

Investors with high cash positions who can absorb elevated insurance premiums without leveraged financing constraints. At a sale price significantly below listing, with a low-leverage or no-leverage structure, the insurance overhang changes the math relative to a fully mortgaged investor competing against rising carrying costs.

Buyers with long time horizons, high risk tolerance, and specific affinity for the Southwest Florida lifestyle. Cape Coral has genuine advantages: canal-front access, warm climate, proximity to Fort Myers. Those are not going away. What changed is the price of entry and the carrying cost of ownership.

Speculative distressed-asset investors who study Florida insurance market dynamics closely and can identify specific property types or locations that are less exposed to the worst insurance premium escalation. This is not a strategy for generalists.

## What This Means for Investors and Buyers

**For distressed-market specialists:** Cape Coral is one of the most discounted markets in Florida. The sale-to-list ratio of 95.88%, a 25.34% price cut rate, and 83-day average marketing time all indicate buyer leverage that did not exist in 2021 and 2022. The 24.37% year-over-year increase in home sales suggests the floor may be in. Investors with deep local knowledge and insurance-cost awareness may find specific opportunities.

**For standard buy-and-hold investors:** A score of 25 with a -9.1% annual price decline and a 31.6% overvaluation estimate is not a standard entry condition. The gross rent yield of approximately 6.6% is attractive on paper but requires careful insurance and operational cost modeling before relying on it.

**For homebuyers relocating to Southwest Florida:** The same buyer leverage that benefits investors benefits buyers. Prices are off the peak, sellers are negotiating, and the region's lifestyle attributes remain intact. Buyers who can afford to hold through continued uncertainty and who have modeled insurance costs specific to their target properties will find more room to negotiate in 2026 than at any point since 2019.

**For investors considering exit:** PropertyIQ does not make sale recommendations. A score of 25 indicates the market remains under pressure, but the recovery trajectory from 9 to 25 suggests the worst may have passed. Sellers considering exit should weigh current buyer leverage against the cost of holding through continued correction versus recovery.

The score of 25 is the most honest data point about Cape Coral in 2026. It is below average. It is recovering. Both things are true.
