## Why Virginia Beach Holds a 91

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metropolitan area, also called Hampton Roads, is home to the largest concentration of military installations in the world. Naval Station Norfolk is the largest naval base on the planet. Joint Base Langley-Eustis, Naval Air Station Oceana, and dozens of supporting commands add hundreds of thousands of military and civilian defense workers to the demand base.

This creates a structural floor under housing demand that very few U.S. metros can match. Military families rotate in and out on permanent change-of-station orders regardless of interest rates. Government civilian employees anchor long-term employment. Defense contractors add a private-sector layer that moves with the federal budget, not the economic cycle.

That is why the score has stayed between 82 and 91 for twelve months. The demand base does not disappear.

## Homes Are Selling at Full Price in 40 Days

Days on market in Virginia Beach is 40 as of February 2026. That is fast. The sale-to-list ratio is 100% as of November 2025. Sellers are receiving full asking price.

Only 13.7% of listings took a price cut as of February 2026, one of the lower readings among markets in this analysis.

These three numbers together describe a market where sellers have leverage, buyers are competitive, and the transaction clears quickly.

## Supply Is Essentially Flat

For-sale inventory rose just 0.33% year-over-year to 3,934 homes as of February 2026. That is essentially unchanged. New listings rose 7.62% to 2,146.

In a metro of 1.78 million people, 3,934 active listings is a tight supply pool. The inventory-to-sales relationship is favorable to sellers and to long-term holders.

Home sales grew 6.49% year-over-year as of February 2026. New construction sales totaled 192 units as of November 2025.

## Rents and the Investment Case

The Zillow Observed Rent Index for Virginia Beach was $1,770 per month as of December 31, 2025. At a home value of $362,358, this produces a gross rent multiplier of approximately 17.1x. That is a reasonable yield for a coastal market.

Military renters provide a unique advantage: they move on government orders, typically give notice, and are generally reliable tenants. Military housing allowance is indexed to local cost of living. For landlords in Virginia Beach, that demand base is durable.

The metro is large enough to support a diversified local economy beyond the defense sector, including healthcare, retail, and tourism. The Virginia Beach oceanfront and the tourism economy contribute to seasonal demand dynamics that sophisticated investors account for in submarket selection.
