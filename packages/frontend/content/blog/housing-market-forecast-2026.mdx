# Housing Market Forecast 2026: What AI and Data Tell Us About the Year Ahead

The **housing market forecast for 2026** is shaping up to be one of the most nuanced in recent memory. After several years of whiplash — from pandemic-era bidding wars to the mortgage rate shock of 2023-2024 — the market is entering a period of recalibration. Inventory is rising in some regions while remaining stubbornly tight in others. Mortgage rates have settled into a new normal. And buyers, sellers, and investors are all trying to figure out what comes next.

At PropertyIQ, we use AI-powered analytics and over 40 economic indicators to forecast market conditions at the metro, county, and ZIP code level. Here is what our models — and the data — are telling us about the housing market in 2026.

## National Overview: A Market in Transition

The national housing market in 2026 is best described as **bifurcated**. Aggregate numbers mask enormous regional variation. Nationally, home prices are projected to grow between 2% and 4% year-over-year, a return to something closer to the long-run average after years of double-digit swings.

Several macro forces are driving this moderation:

- **Mortgage rates** have stabilized in the 5.8%-6.4% range after the Federal Reserve's measured rate cuts in late 2025. This is lower than the 2023 peak but well above the sub-3% pandemic lows that fueled the boom.
- **Inventory** is climbing nationally, with active listings up roughly 12% year-over-year in early 2026. However, inventory growth is concentrated in Sun Belt markets while the Northeast and Midwest remain supply-constrained.
- **New construction** is filling some of the gap. Single-family housing starts have recovered to pre-pandemic levels in markets with available land, particularly in Texas, Florida, and the Carolinas.
- **Affordability** remains the central challenge. The typical monthly mortgage payment is still 35-40% higher than it was in early 2022, even with modest price corrections in some metros.

## Key Metrics to Watch in 2026

Our housing market forecast for 2026 is built on tracking a specific set of leading indicators. These are the metrics that historically move *before* prices do:

### Months of Supply
The single most predictive metric for price direction. Markets with fewer than 3 months of supply tend to see price appreciation; markets above 5 months tend to see price softening. Nationally, we are hovering around 3.8 months — balanced territory — but the metro-level picture varies wildly.

### Price-to-Income Ratio
Affordability pressure is the governor on price growth. Markets where the median home price exceeds 5x the median household income face structural headwinds. Our models weight this metric heavily when forecasting price ceilings.

### Days on Market
Rising days on market is an early signal of softening demand. We are seeing DOM climb in markets like [Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler, AZ](/markets/phoenix-mesa-chandler-az) and [Austin, TX](/markets/austin-tx) while remaining compressed in supply-starved metros like [Hartford, CT](/markets/hartford-east-hartford-middletown-ct) and [Providence, RI](/markets/providence-warwick-ri).

### Net Migration and Job Growth
Population flows and employment trends are the fundamental demand drivers. Markets with strong in-migration and job growth — particularly in healthcare, technology, and logistics — are positioned for sustained demand.

## Regional Hotspots: Where the Action Is

The housing market forecast for 2026 is fundamentally a story of geography. Here are the regional patterns our models are flagging:

### Sun Belt: Cooling After the Boom
Markets like [Austin, TX](/markets/austin-tx), [Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler, AZ](/markets/phoenix-mesa-chandler-az), and [Boise City-Nampa, ID](/markets/boise-city-nampa-id) experienced explosive growth during the pandemic migration wave, followed by meaningful corrections. In 2026, these markets are finding their footing. Inventory has normalized, speculative excess has been wrung out, and valuations are more sustainable. Our models show these markets as **transitioning from correction to stabilization**, with flat-to-modest price growth expected through the year.

### Southeast: Sustained Momentum
[Nashville-Davidson-Murfreesboro-Franklin, TN](/markets/nashville-davidson-murfreesboro-franklin-tn), [Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia, NC](/markets/charlotte-concord-gastonia-nc), and [Raleigh-Cary, NC](/markets/raleigh-cary-nc) continue to benefit from strong job growth, corporate relocations, and relative affordability compared to coastal metros. These markets have absorbed new supply without significant price pressure and remain among our highest-scoring metros for both homebuyers and investors.

### South Florida: Watch Closely
[Miami-Fort Lauderdale, FL](/markets/miami-fort-lauderdale-fl) and [Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater, FL](/markets/tampa-st-petersburg-clearwater-fl) present a mixed picture. International demand remains a floor under luxury pricing, but the condo market faces headwinds from rising insurance costs, HOA special assessments driven by post-Surfside regulations, and a surge of new construction deliveries. Our models are flagging elevated risk in the South Florida condo segment specifically.

### Midwest and Northeast: Quietly Strong
Some of the best risk-adjusted opportunities in 2026 are in markets that rarely make headlines. [Columbus, OH](/markets/columbus-oh), [Indianapolis-Carmel-Anderson, IN](/markets/indianapolis-carmel-anderson-in), and [Kansas City, MO-KS](/markets/kansas-city-mo-ks) offer strong price-to-income ratios, stable employment bases, and inventory levels that support moderate appreciation. These markets score well on our [HomeReady](/scores) metric because fundamentals are solid and entry prices are accessible.
