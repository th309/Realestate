Philadelphia is the sixth-largest metropolitan area in the United States, home to 6.24 million people. It sits between two of the most expensive real estate markets in the country. New York and Boston dominate conversations about Northeast investing. Philadelphia rarely does.

[PropertyIQ scores Philadelphia a 70 out of 100](/markets/philadelphia-camden-wilmington-pa-nj-de-md) as of February 28, 2026. That is a C- grade, reflecting a market with real structural strengths alongside meaningful headwinds. The score has held in the 67 to 76 range for the past twelve months.

Here is what the data shows.

## The Affordability Gap That Defines Philadelphia

The median listing price in Philadelphia is $356,425 as of February 2026. The Zillow estimated home value is approximately $375,224 as of January 2026.

Compare that to its Northeast neighbors: Boston metro median values regularly exceed $600,000. New York metro median values exceed $750,000. Philadelphia, with a population base comparable to greater Boston, prices its homes at roughly half the cost.

At $226 per square foot as of February 2026, Philadelphia offers density, infrastructure, and a major labor market at a fraction of what those same inputs cost in other Northeast metros.

This pricing gap is not primarily a function of economic weakness. Median household income in the Philadelphia metro is $89,273 as of 2023 Census data. That is a strong income base, comparable to or above many Sun Belt markets that carry significantly higher home prices.

The income required to purchase a home at current prices is $94,736 as of February 2026. At $89,273 median income, the gap between what households earn and what they need to comfortably buy is narrow relative to most major metros. The estimated affordable home price for median-income households is $335,870 as of February 2026.

The market is 19.8% overvalued relative to income fundamentals as of February 2026. That premium is real but far below the 40% to 60% overvaluation figures seen in many Sun Belt markets at their peak.

## Supply Dynamics: A Mixed Picture

For-sale inventory in Philadelphia is 9,095 active listings as of February 2026, up 4.16% year over year. That supply increase is modest given the metro's size and puts Philadelphia in a more balanced range than the severely constrained markets scoring in the 90s nationally.

New listings are actually declining: down 5.87% year over year as of February 2026, with 4,872 new properties entering the market. This divergence -- rising total inventory alongside falling new listings -- suggests that homes are sitting longer while fresh supply slows.

Days on market is 53 as of February 2026. That is not a frantic pace, but homes are not accumulating either.

The pending ratio stands at 69.79% as of February 2026, with 6,348 pending listings against 9,095 active. Demand is absorbing supply at a consistent rate. Sellers are not facing a bidding crisis, but buyers are not holding significant leverage either.

## Sellers Are Getting Their Price

The sale-to-list ratio in Philadelphia is 100% as of November 2025. Homes are selling at asking price on average. Only 11.13% of listings have taken a price cut as of February 2026.

This is noteworthy given that the overall market score is 70, not 90. Philadelphia is not a seller's market in terms of extreme demand pressure, but sellers are not being forced to discount. The market is balanced enough that buyers are paying asking.

Home values have risen 1.84% year over year as of February 2026. Five-year appreciation is 11.38% as of February 2026. That is modest compared to the Sun Belt run-up of 2020 to 2023, but it also means Philadelphia did not experience the correction that followed. The market did not overshoot, so it does not have to correct.

## The Rent Profile

The Zillow Observed Rent Index for Philadelphia is approximately $1,849 per month as of December 2025. The metro's rent-for-houses percentile ranks at 62 among comparable metro areas as of December 2025.

With home values in the $350,000 to $375,000 range and rents approaching $1,850 per month, Philadelphia offers a rent-to-price ratio that is more competitive than coastal markets but less favorable than Midwest metros like Grand Rapids or Rochester.

New construction activity is measured: 242 new construction sales as of November 2025. The supply pipeline is not flooding the market with competing inventory.

## Economic and Demographic Foundation

Unemployment in the Philadelphia metro is 4.0% as of December 2025. The metro anchors a major healthcare, education, financial services, and logistics economy. Major employers include University of Pennsylvania Health System, Jefferson Health, Comcast, and a substantial pharmaceutical cluster in the surrounding counties.

Population is 6.24 million as of 2023 Census data. Median age is 39.1. Homeownership rate is 66.99%.

These are not the demographics of a market at risk of demand collapse. Philadelphia's population base is stable, diversified, and economically productive.

## The Score in Context

A 70 reflects a market that works. It is not the explosive-growth reading that scores above 85 produce. It is not a warning signal like scores below 40. It is a functioning major metro with real estate fundamentals that support steady activity.

For buyers interested in Northeast proximity without Northeast pricing, Philadelphia is the data-defined option. For investors focused on cash flow over appreciation trajectory, the rent-to-price relationships are worth modeling at the property level.

As always, the PropertyIQ Score reflects market-level conditions as of February 28, 2026. It is one input, not a substitute for property-specific analysis and individual financial evaluation.
