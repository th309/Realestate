Reno, Nevada scores a 64 out of 100 on the PropertyIQ index as of February 28, 2026. That number by itself is a middle-of-the-road reading. The story is the direction: three months ago it was 41. In December 2025 it was 42. The score has risen 23 points in 90 days.

[PropertyIQ scores Reno a 64 out of 100](/markets/reno-nv) as of February 28, 2026. Here is what is driving the move.

## Inventory Is Contracting While Sales Are Accelerating

The structural shift in Reno is happening on both sides of the supply-demand equation simultaneously.

For-sale inventory dropped 12.18% year-over-year to 1,075 active listings as of February 2026. New listings fell 6.98% year-over-year to 586 units. Fewer sellers are listing, and the available pool is shrinking.

On the demand side, home sales rose 12.53% year-over-year as of February 2026. More buyers, fewer listings.

The result shows up in the pending ratio: 750 homes under contract against 1,075 active listings as of February 2026, a pending ratio of 69.8%. For every 100 homes on the market, 70 already have buyers. That is a tight market.

## Home Values Are Rising at a Rate That Demands Attention

The median listing price in Reno is $636,800 as of February 2026. The Zillow estimated home value is $557,040 as of January 31, 2026.

Home values rose 11.23% year-over-year as of February 2026. That is one of the higher appreciation rates in the Western U.S. for a market of this size at this stage of the rate cycle. Month-over-month, values rose 1.89%.

The five-year appreciation figure is 31.98% as of February 2026. Reno has been appreciating steadily, driven by California out-migration, Nevada's tax advantages, and a diversifying technology and logistics employment base.

The PropertyIQ Score for Reno shows a sharp recovery from a trough:

Scores in the low-to-mid 30s typically reflect supply excess, price softness, or demand withdrawal. The reversal to 64 in four months reflects the inventory contraction and sales acceleration described above.

## The Affordability Tension

Reno's main constraint is cost. At $557,040 for a median home and a median household income of $84,684 (Census, 2023), the income gap is significant. The estimated income required to purchase at current prices is $169,259. The estimated affordable home price for a median-income household is $318,605.

The overvaluation reading is 36.5% as of February 2026. That is elevated but not extreme by Nevada standards. The state's tax advantages, particularly no state income tax, partially offset the price premium for relocating buyers from California.

Price per square foot is $332 as of February 2026. Price cuts affect 13.1% of listings, one of the lower readings in this batch and consistent with a market where sellers have pricing power.

Sale-to-list ratio is 98.8% as of November 2025. Days on market is 72, which is longer than the tightest markets but consistent with a high-price environment where buyers take more time.

## Rent and Investment Fundamentals

The Zillow Observed Rent Index for Reno was $1,887 per month as of December 31, 2025. At a home value of $557,040, this is a thin yield. Cash-flow investing in Reno requires significant equity or exceptional submarket selection.

The investment case for Reno is primarily an appreciation play: inventory contracting, values rising at 11% annually, a recovering score, and a demand base supported by California migration and Nevada tax policy.

Zillow's 12-month home price forecast as of December 2025 is +1.3%. That is conservative relative to the trailing 11% and may lag the inventory-driven momentum currently building.

New construction sales totaled 18 units as of November 2025. That is a notably constrained new supply pipeline for a metro of 556,539 people (Census, 2023).

## The Employment Context

Unemployment in Reno was 4.2% as of November 2025, slightly above the national rate. The economy has diversified significantly from its historical casino base: Amazon, Tesla (Gigafactory), and Google have established large operations in the broader Reno-Sparks area. The homeownership rate is 61.61% as of 2023, reflecting a more renter-heavy market than Midwest metros.

## What the Score Does Not Tell You

A PropertyIQ Score of 64 reflects current market conditions as of February 28, 2026. It measures supply, demand, price trends, economic fundamentals, and inventory dynamics. A score of 64 rising from 34 is a different market than a score of 64 declining from 85. The direction matters.

Any buyer or investor evaluating Reno should conduct their own due diligence on local submarket conditions, financing assumptions, and personal investment objectives before making any decisions.

## The Bottom Line

Reno scores 64 out of 100 on the PropertyIQ index as of February 28, 2026, up 23 points in three months. Inventory is contracting at 12% year-over-year. Home values rose 11.23% in the past year. The pending ratio is near 70%.

At $557,000 median home value, this is not an affordable market. The investment thesis is appreciation and migration-driven demand, not cash flow. The score momentum says that thesis has reactivated.
