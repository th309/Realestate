# Topic: Building AI Market Reports

> **DRAFT — pending Troy's vetting.** Do not generate from this doc until reviewed.

Audience: everyone — buyers, investors, and agents building something to send a
client.
Angle: the report builder turns a market into a written, cited, shareable
document. You pick a template, pick a market, answer a few questions about
yourself, and the report is written around your situation instead of being a
generic data dump.

Every template, wizard step, and export path named below exists today under the
name shown. All numbers must come from the approved-claims rules in README.md.

## The hook

"Pick a market. Answer four questions. Get a written market report that argues a
position and shows its work — then send the link to your client."

## Eight report tasks (one graphic each)

1. **Pick the right template** — there are five, and picking correctly is most
   of the job. Market Snapshot is a quick pulse on a single market. Market
   Comparison puts two to five markets side by side. Investment Analysis is the
   investor deep dive with a pro forma. Affordability & Migration covers
   demographics and population flow. Market Cycle & Risk covers cycle position
   and scenarios.
2. **Tell it who you are** — the builder has a reader-type switch for
   Homebuyer/Renter, Investor, and Real Estate Agent. The same market produces a
   different report depending on which you pick, because the questions that
   matter are different.
3. **Choose the market and level** — reports run at National, State, Metro,
   County, City, and ZIP Code level. Pick the level that matches the decision:
   metro for a relocation, ZIP for a rental.
4. **Answer the personalization questions** — the builder asks for your inputs
   before writing, so the narrative can reason about your actual situation
   rather than an average one.
5. **Reorder the sections before you generate** — sections are drag-and-drop, so
   the report leads with whatever your reader cares about most. Lead a client
   report with the verdict; lead your own with the data.
6. **Read what the report actually contains** — a generated report opens with
   the market's PropertyIQ Score, then moves through AI-written narrative
   sections and data blocks: metric grids, percentile rank against peers, score
   breakdown, forecast, strengths and risks, and, for the investment template, a
   full pro forma with cash flow, returns, assumptions, and sensitivity.
   Comparison reports add a side-by-side table with per-category winners.
7. **Ask the report follow-up questions** — every report has a conversation
   panel. You can ask things like "What does this score mean for me?", "Is now a
   good time to buy?", "What are the main risks?", or "How does this compare to
   national averages?" and get an answer grounded in that report's data.
8. **Send it out** — export to PDF, or generate a public view-only share link
   that anyone can open without an account. CSV export of the underlying data is
   a paid feature.

## Approved claims (use these exact framings)

- Coverage: 900+ metros, 3,000+ counties, 29,000+ ZIPs, scored monthly.
- The PropertyIQ Score leading each report is a 1-99 momentum and timing signal;
  50 = the market's state average; higher scores have historically outperformed
  relative to their state. Momentum words only. A/B/C/F letters are
  data-confidence, never a score grade.
- Data sources: Zillow, Realtor.com, Census, FRED, BLS.
- Narrative sections are AI-written from that market's real data. Say
  "AI-written from live market data", not "AI-generated insights" as a vague
  claim.

## What NOT to claim

- Reports are market-level. They do not value a specific property; that is the
  Deal Analyzer, and it is a different tool. Do not merge the two in one
  graphic.
- Do not promise a number of reports per plan, or name a price. Tier packaging
  changes; the topic doc does not track it.
- Do not show a report section that belongs to a different template. The pro
  forma blocks are Investment Analysis; the winners table is Market Comparison.
