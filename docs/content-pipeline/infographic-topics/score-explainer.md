# Topic: The PropertyIQ Score, Honestly Explained

> **DRAFT — pending Troy's vetting.** Do not generate from this doc until reviewed.

Audience: everyone — investors, agents, and first-time buyers.
Angle: the PropertyIQ Score is the product's signature number, and it is the
single most misread thing on the site. It is a **momentum and timing signal**,
not a quality grade. These infographics exist to make one honest point at a
time so the number never gets read as "good market / bad market".

## The hook

"It's a 1 to 99 score, and 50 means average — for that market's state. It tells
you where demand is heading right now. It does not tell you whether a place is
a good place."

## Eight things the score means (one graphic each)

1. **What the number is** — a 1 to 99 momentum and timing signal for a market's
   demand. 50 is the market's state average. Higher scores have historically
   outperformed relative to that market's own state. Read it as a direction of
   travel, not a rating out of 100.
2. **How to read the momentum label** — every score carries a momentum word and
   a direction arrow. The full ladder: Very Strong (90+, up), Strong (80-89,
   up), Rising (70-79, up), Firming (60-69, up), Steady (50-59, sideways),
   Easing (40-49, easing), Weak (20-39, down), Very Weak (below 20, down). These
   eight words are the whole vocabulary.
3. **A low score is not a bad market** — the correction graphic. A low score
   means demand momentum is cooling right now; it says nothing about whether a
   market is desirable, liveable, or worth owning. Cooling momentum is
   information about timing. Never render a low score as a failing market.
4. **The letter is about the data, not the score** — A, B, C, and F are
   confidence grades describing data quality and coverage, and they are fully
   independent of the score itself. A means 80-100% coverage and freshness; B is
   65-79%, good data with minor gaps; C is 45-64%, fair data with notable gaps;
   F is 0-44%, insufficient data. A market can score 88 with a C, or 22 with an
   A. The letter is never a grade on the score.
5. **The four inputs** — the score is built from exactly four signals: 12-month
   home value momentum and 3-month home value momentum, both from Zillow's ZHVI
   (rising values push the score up); plus median days on market and the share
   of listings with price cuts, both from Realtor.com (rising values push the
   score down). No Redfin data enters the score. A market needs at least two of
   the four to be scored at all, and how many of the four are present is what
   sets the confidence letter.
6. **Computed nationally, calibrated to a state** — both are true, and they
   answer different questions. Each of the four signals is normalized and ranked
   across every market at that geography level nationwide, so the comparison
   pool is national. The resulting scale is then calibrated so that 50 lands on
   state-average performance, which is what makes "higher score, outperforms its
   state" the right way to read it. The score is not ranked within a state.
7. **What the score has actually predicted** — the validation graphic. Metros
   scoring in the top band have historically outperformed their state benchmark;
   metros in the bottom band have underperformed it. Use only the approved
   figures in the claims section below, with their labels attached.
8. **Where you can get one** — coverage. Scores are published monthly for 900+
   metros, 3,000+ counties, and 29,000+ ZIP codes, with history backfilled to 2001. Every scored market has a page; the map colors them; the reports cite
   them.

## Approved claims (use these exact framings)

- Coverage: 900+ metros, 3,000+ counties, 29,000+ ZIPs, scored monthly. Never
  write a raw scored count.
- Scale: 1-99. 50 = the market's state average. The zero-crossing is 50 at every
  geography level.
- Historical performance, metro level, full-formula era (2016 onward), 865
  metros — this denominator must stay attached to these numbers:
  - Top band (81-99): +0.38 percentage points annualized 3-year excess return
    versus state.
  - Bottom band (1-20): -1.29 percentage points on the same basis.
  - Top-scored markets beat their state roughly 56% of the time over 3-year
    horizons; bottom-scored markets roughly 37% of the time.
- Data sources for the score: Zillow and Realtor.com only. Broader platform data
  also draws on Census, FRED, and BLS, but those do not enter the score.
- Language rules: momentum words only. Never Excellent, Good, Poor, Bad, Best,
  Worst, or any grade word applied to the score. Never call A/B/C/F a score
  grade. Never say the score is ranked within a state.
