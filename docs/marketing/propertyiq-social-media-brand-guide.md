## 1. North Star: What Success Actually Looks Like

Rank metrics in this order. Don't optimize for the bottom of this list at the expense of the top.

1. **Signups (paid account creation at propertyiq.app)** — this is the only metric that matters commercially. Every post should have a clear, specific path to a signup.
2. **Site sessions / map & report interactions from social traffic** — the leading indicator for #1. If a post doesn't drive click-throughs, it's not working even if it "performs" on-platform.
3. **Shares / reposts / saves** — the multiplier. A share puts the post in front of a warm, trusting audience for free. This is how I get more of #1 and #2 without paid spend.
4. **Followers** — a byproduct, not a goal. Follower count with no signups or shares means the content is generic enough to watch but not valuable enough to act on.

**The implication for content:** every post must stand alone as genuinely useful before it asks for anything. Don't write ads. Write the kind of data-driven insight a real estate investor or agent would screenshot and text to a friend — then give them a specific, low-friction reason to click through for "the rest of it" (their market, the full ranking, the full report).

**CTA discipline:**

- Never "Sign up now" — always tie the CTA to what was just teased: _"See [Metro]'s full score breakdown — free, no credit card" beats "Learn more."
- Deep-link to the specific page (that market's report, the score methodology page, the live map) rather than the homepage. Less friction = more conversions.
- Use UTM parameters per post/platform so signups can be attributed back to what drove them (Google Analytics is already connected on the backend — ask Troy for reporting access rather than guessing at attribution).

## 4. Brand Voice & Tone

### 4.1 Voice attributes

| Attribute          | Meaning                                                               |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Confident**      | State findings directly. Not tentative, not hedging.                  |
| **Conversational** | Like a knowledgeable friend, not a textbook or a sales pitch.         |
| **Data-first**     | Lead with a number, a percentage, a trend — not an opinion or a vibe. |
| **Accessible**     | Explain plainly. No unexplained jargon.                               |
| **Actionable**     | Always point to a next step or decision.                              |

Internal shorthand used by the AI content system: **"confident, conversational, data-first, not hypey."**

### 4.2 Do / Don't

| Do                                                  | Don't                                        |
| --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| "The data shows 12% rent growth in this metro"      | "Our proprietary algorithm detected..."      |
| "Dallas–Fort Worth scored 78 — strong fundamentals" | "This is an amazing investment opportunity!" |
| "Explore this market"                               | "Buy now!" / "Don't miss out!"               |
| "B-level confidence — good data, rental comps thin" | "We're not totally sure about this one"      |
| "Your PropertyIQ report is ready"                   | "CONGRATULATIONS on your new report!"        |

### 4.3 Banned words and patterns (hard rule — content is automatically rejected internally for these)

Never use: _game-changer, crushing it, absolutely_ (as an intensifier), _no-brainer, you won't believe, insane, crazy good, literally_ (as filler), _tbh, omg_.

**No em dashes or en dashes** (—, –) anywhere in copy. Use a period, comma, or colon instead.

**No unsupported superlatives** ("the hottest/best/fastest/most...") unless a specific data point in the same post backs it up.

**Never name competitors in public posts.** Positioning against them (Section 8) is for your own content strategy, not for callouts, comparisons, or subtweeting in copy.

### 4.4 Terminology

| Use This                     | Not This                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| PropertyIQ Score / PIQ Score | "Score" on first mention (establish the full name first, then "the Score" is fine after) |
| Confidence level             | Accuracy / Trust score                                                                   |
| Market intelligence          | Market report / Analysis                                                                 |
| Geography / Market           | Area / Zone / Region                                                                     |

### 4.5 How to talk about the PropertyIQ Score (read carefully — this is the part most likely to get misquoted)

- It's a **1–99 momentum score**, recalculated monthly, built from: home-price momentum (12-month and 3-month), median days on market, and share of listings with a price cut.
- **It predicts where a market is headed, not whether it's a "good" or "bad" market.** A low score means cooling momentum, not a low-quality market. Never use quality words like "excellent," "good," "poor," or "bad" to describe a score — only momentum language: rising, steady, easing, weak, etc.
- **50 = that market's own state average.** A score above 50 means a market is expected to outperform its state peers; below 50, underperform.
- Confidence (shown as a letter grade A/B/C/F) is a **data-quality** indicator — how much good data went into the score — and is completely separate from the score itself. A market can have a great score and a lower confidence grade if data is thin, or vice versa.
- **Never say** the score is "ranked within its state" or "computed relative to its state" — it's computed nationally across all markets, then the scale is calibrated so 50 lands on each market's state average. Nationally computed, state-calibrated — both true, don't conflate them, and don't say only one.
- **Approved coverage stat for public use:** "900+ metros, 3,000+ counties, 29,000+ ZIPs" scored. Don't use any other coverage number you find in older internal docs or drafts — this is the current, approved figure. If in doubt, ask Troy before publishing a specific stat.

## 5. What PropertyIQ Actually Does (for your own understanding, and to draw content ideas from)

PropertyIQ is a real estate market intelligence platform. Users get:

- An interactive **map** of every U.S. metro, county, and ZIP code, colored by the PropertyIQ Score
- **AI-generated market reports** — a written narrative analysis for any market, generated fresh, not templated
- Historical trend charts, rent data, and (on paid tiers) CSV export
- A **Claude (MCP) integration** — PropertyIQ is one of the only products in its category with a working AI-assistant integration, letting a user ask Claude directly for PropertyIQ market data. This is a genuine differentiator worth its own content (see Section 8).

## 8. Content Strategy: Make Every Post Worth Sharing

The mandate from Section 1 — signups first, shares as the multiplier — means content can't just be an ad with a logo on it. It has to teach or reveal something real, then point to the site for "the rest of it." Below are content pillars built around that.

### Pillar A — Market Score Reveals (monthly, tied to the score refresh)

A single market's score, presented as a real finding: _"Austin just scored 82 this month. Here's what that means for the next 6–12 months."_ Use the score-ring visual style (indigo circle, bold number) for a scroll-stopping, personal-feeling graphic — people share things about _their own_ city. CTA: _"Check your market's score free at propertyiq.app/map."_

### Pillar B — Top/Bottom N Rankings ("countdown" format)

A ranked list format ("10 metros beating their state average this month") in a declarative, sparse tone — think Apple keynote, not clickbait. This format is already scripted internally for video (see `content-pipeline/prompts/top_10_ranking.md`) with a fixed close: _"Learn more at propertyiq.app."_ Naturally suited to LinkedIn carousels, YouTube Shorts, and TikTok — countdown formats get watched to the end and shared.

### Pillar C — Explainers / "how this actually works"

Teach one concept per post: what "days on market" means and why it matters, what the confidence grade means, why 50 = state average. This is where you differentiate on trust without ever naming a competitor — PropertyIQ publishes its methodology and shows its confidence grade; most competitors in this space don't show either. Let the content imply that by being transparent, not by contrasting directly.

### Pillar D — Agent Toolkit spotlights

Show (don't just tell) an agent using a PropertyIQ score + AI narrative in a listing presentation. This is aimed squarely at Audience #2 (Section 2) — agents are likeliest to reshare content that makes them look sharp in front of their own clients.

### Pillar E — "Two Markets, Opposite Futures" comparisons

A head-to-head: two metros, same setup, diverging score trajectories. Naturally invites shares and tags ("which one are you watching?").

### CTA principles (repeat of Section 1, applied per pillar)

- Tie the CTA to exactly what was just shown (that market's report, that ranking's full list, the score methodology page) — not a generic homepage link.
- Always frame the free tier explicitly: "free," "no credit card required."
- Use per-post/per-platform UTM tags so signups can be traced back to content.

## 10. Competitive Positioning (internal — informs tone, don't put this in public posts)

Don't name any of these competitors publicly. Use this only to understand _why_ PropertyIQ's content should lean the way it does in Section 8.

- **BiggerPockets** — a media company with a huge audience but stale, MSA-only market data. PropertyIQ's edge: fresher, more granular (down to ZIP) data.
- **Reventure App** — PropertyIQ's closest structural peer (map + score product), but its score has no published methodology or confidence indicator, and its content tone leans "perma-bear." PropertyIQ's edge: a published, transparent methodology and a neutral tone — "the score, not the story."
- **Mashvisor** — no visible accuracy/confidence disclosure. PropertyIQ's edge: transparency.
- **PropStream** — a lead-gen/skip-tracing tool, not a direct competitor. PropertyIQ intentionally doesn't compete here; it's the "where to look" step before a tool like this.
- **DealCheck** — strong deal-analyzer UX but no market-level intelligence. PropertyIQ's edge: connecting a specific deal to the market context around it ("this deal cash-flows _and_ sits in a 78-score ZIP").

**The throughline for content:** PropertyIQ's core credibility angle is transparency — a real methodology, a visible confidence grade, no black box. That's a content well you can return to often (Pillar C) without ever needing to mention a competitor by name.
