Frequently Asked Questions

What is the PropertyIQ market screener?

The market screener lets you filter and rank every scored PropertyIQ market by PropertyIQ Score, price, cap rate, months of supply, and overvaluation, across metro, county, and ZIP geography levels. It turns thousands of markets into a sortable table so you can find the ones that match a specific investing or buying strategy in seconds.

What can I filter markets by in the screener?

You can set a minimum and maximum range for PropertyIQ Score, median price, cap rate, months of supply, and overvalued percentage, then combine any of those with a state filter to narrow results further. A market size toggle also lets you hide smaller metros, counties, and ZIPs so the table defaults to markets with enough population to matter, while still letting you switch it off to see every market size.

What quick-start presets does the screener offer?

Five one-click presets are available. Hottest Markets sorts by PropertyIQ Score, Undervalued + High Score surfaces markets scoring 70 or above that are not overvalued, and Cash-Flow filters to markets with an estimated cap rate of 6 percent or higher. Biggest Gainers and Biggest Losers sort markets by how much their PropertyIQ Score has changed over the selected time window.

What is the difference between the Screener tab and the Movers tab?

The Screener tab shows a full sortable, filterable table of every market, while the Movers tab shows two side-by-side leaderboards of the top markets whose PropertyIQ Score rose or fell the most over your selected time window. Both tabs share the same geography level, state filter, and market size setting, and the window can be set to 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 3 years, or 5 years.

Is ZIP-level screening and CSV export free?

Screening at the metro and county level is available on the free tier, but ZIP-level results require a Pro plan, and switching the geography selector to ZIP without Pro access shows an upgrade prompt instead of results. Exporting the current table to CSV is also a Pro feature, so free-tier users see a locked Export CSV button in the page header.

How current is the data in the screener?

Every row in the screener comes from the same monthly snapshot, and the exact date is shown as a Data as of stamp next to the page title. When a new PropertyIQ Score run completes each month, the screener table, its Movers leaderboards, and its CSV export all reflect the new snapshot automatically.

What changed, and why

Restyle only — every filter, column, preset, control and FAQ from the live page is preserved
  1. Collapsed three stacked control rows into one app-level bar. The live page stacks a global nav, a breadcrumb, a Screener/Movers + window row, and a geo + state + market-size + presets row — four bands of chrome before any data appears, pushing the first result row to roughly 440px down the page. Tabs, geography, the Δ window, state, and the market-size toggle now sit in a single light bar under the app bar, so the only chrome above the page title is a 57px app bar and one control row.
  2. Moved the five presets inside the Filters card. On the live page the preset chips sit in the chrome row while the six range inputs hide in a separate collapsed accordion below, so clicking Cash-Flow silently writes capRateMin=6 into a panel you cannot see. Presets and inputs are now one panel, and the preset that wrote the value is visibly active above the input it changed.
  3. Made the applied filter legible. Live feedback is a bare count badge next to the word “Filters” — you get 1, not which one. The panel now shows the filled input in violet plus a removable Cap Rate ≥ 6% chip on the footer row beside Clear all filters.
  4. Rebuilt the score column as a banded pill. Live scores are bare coloured digits on a continuous rainbow ramp: 33, 41 and 46 render as three near-identical oranges, and the warm-to-cool gradient reads as a heat map of market quality. Five discrete pills now map to the momentum bands — 70+ rising/strong, 60–69 firming, 50–59 steady, 40–49 easing, under 40 weak — with 50 (the state average) sitting on brand indigo so the reference point is visible in the scale itself.
  5. Added a legend that names the momentum bands. Nothing on the live screener says what a score of 6 means, and the number alone invites reading it as a grade. The legend spells out the labels and states outright that a low score is cooling momentum, not a bad market — matching what the market detail page shows (Clarksdale, MS: 6, VERY WEAK).
  6. Sticky header, one number format per column, true minus signs. The live header scrolls out of view within one wheel gesture on a 50-row page and never comes back. Every numeric cell is now mono tabular and right-aligned, negatives use − rather than a hyphen, and the header stays pinned while you scroll 50 rows.
  7. Made sortability and sort state visible. Live headers carry a faint double chevron that is easy to miss, and Rent silently has none because it is not sortable. Sortable headers now carry the chevron pair; the active column turns indigo with a single direction arrow, and Rent stays plain so the difference is obvious.
  8. Surfaced the row menu instead of hiding it behind a ⋮. Favorite, View on Map, Generate Report and Set Alert — plus the five metrics an alert can watch — are only discoverable by clicking the overflow dot on a row. The same menu is documented as a panel beside the legend so the actions are visible without a click.
  9. Gave the results a header that states what you are looking at. The live table has no title or result summary above it; the only context is a Showing 1–26 of 26 line under the last row. The card header now names the match count, the universe it came from, and the active sort before the first row.
  10. Kept the FAQ but stopped it running the page width. Six full-width prose cards stretch each answer across the whole page. Two-up above 1240px brings each answer to a readable measure without cutting a word.

Found on the live page

  1. The state filter has no Puerto Rico option, but PR metros are in the results. The select lists 50 states plus DC; Arecibo, PR ranks fourth on the 3M Top Losers board. There is no way to filter to it.
  2. Clicking a market name navigates away with no warning. Market names are links to /market/{id}. Clicking one from a screen you spent time building discards the whole filter and sort state — there is no back-to-results affordance on the destination page.
  3. The default landing state is not neutral. /screener?geo=metro redirects to ?geo=metro&preset=hottest, so the page always opens with a preset already applied — but the Filters badge shows no count, because Hottest Markets only sets a sort, not a filter.
  4. Rent is missing for a third of the Cash-Flow matches. Nine of the 26 rows show a dash for Rent, including the two highest-cap-rate markets (Clarksdale, MS at 13.6% and Greenville, MS at 10.2%) — yet Cap Rate, the very column the preset filters on, still prints a value for every one of them. Nothing on the row says where that cap rate came from.
